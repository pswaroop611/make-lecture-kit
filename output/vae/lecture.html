Variational
Autoencoders

From basic autoencoders to probabilistic generative models. We will explore how to tame the messy, irregular latent space of an autoencoder by predicting distributions, penalizing with KL divergence, and ultimately generating novel images.

01 · Foundations

Autoencoder (Recap)

Autoencoders compress data into a smaller representation and then try to reconstruct it. But standard autoencoders just want to copy the input. They do not care how the latent space is organized, leading to an irregular, patchy map.

Analogy first. An autoencoder is like packing a suitcase in a rush. You cram everything in to make it fit (encoding) and pull it out when you arrive (decoding). But the space inside the suitcase is a mess. If you reach into a random spot, you might pull out a shoe mixed with a toothbrush.

Step 1 — The bottleneck

The network has an encoder that shrinks the input into a latent space (\(z\)), and a decoder that blows it back up to the original input (\(x\)). It forces the system to keep only the most vital details.

Step 2 — The gap problem

Standard autoencoders lose "semantics" in the gaps. We want to be able to pick any point in the latent space and decode it into something that looks real. But a standard autoencoder gives us an irregular latent space.

Walk it (The gap)

Let's encode an image of a cat to \(z_1 = [0, 1]\) and a dog to \(z_2 = [1, 0]\).

Where this shows up in ML

Basic autoencoders are used for denoising images or anomaly detection (anomalies reconstruct poorly), but for generation, we need to enforce structure, leading us to Variational Autoencoders.

Pitfall — sampling fails. Standard autoencoders are great for compression, but terrible for generation. You cannot just sample random numbers and expect a realistic output because the space is full of holes.

Bridge → To fix this gap problem, we need a way to measure and control the shape of the latent space. Enter Entropy and KL-Divergence.

02 · Foundations

Entropy & KL-Divergence

To fix the autoencoder, we need a way to measure and control the shape of our latent space. We use two ideas from information theory: Entropy and KL-Divergence.

Analogy first. Think of entropy as the clutter in your room. A perfectly organized room has zero entropy. KL-Divergence is like comparing your room's layout to a picture in an IKEA catalog. The bigger the difference, the higher the penalty.

Step 1 — Entropy

Entropy is the expected value of information for an event. It measures disorder. For a pure, perfectly predictable system, entropy is zero.

Step 2 — KL-Divergence

KL-Divergence (Kullback-Leibler Divergence) measures the difference in information content between two distributions, \(p\) and \(q\).

$$ D_{KL}(p \parallel q) = \mathbb{E}_{p(x)} \left[ \log \frac{p(x)}{q(x)} \right] $$

Here, the expectation \(\mathbb{E}\) is computed with respect to \(p(x)\). KL-Divergence is always positive, and it is not symmetric: \(D_{KL}(p \parallel q)\) is not the same as \(D_{KL}(q \parallel p)\).

Walk it (KL-Divergence of two simple probabilities)

Let \(p\) and \(q\) be two distributions over two states, \(A\) and \(B\). Let \(p(A) = 0.5, p(B) = 0.5\). Let \(q(A) = 0.8, q(B) = 0.2\).

⚡ Divergence

Move the "model" distribution (pink) closer to the "target" distribution (cyan). Watch how the KL Divergence drops to zero when they perfectly overlap.

Where this shows up in ML

KL-Divergence is the core of the Variational Autoencoder loss function. It acts as a regularizer, forcing the network's latent groupings to match a standard Gaussian distribution, ensuring no gaps.

Pitfall — not a true distance. Never treat KL-Divergence as a true "distance" metric, because it is not symmetric. The order matters!

Bridge → Now that we know how to measure the difference between distributions, let's use it to fix our autoencoder.

03 · Variational Autoencoders

Variational Autoencoders (VAE)

A Variational Autoencoder (VAE) solves the gap problem by changing what the encoder outputs. Instead of outputting a single point, the encoder outputs parameters of a distribution. Usually this is the mean and variance of a Gaussian curve.

Analogy first. A VAE draws a circle and says the arrow will land inside it. The circles overlap. There are no empty spots on the target.

Step 1 — Distribution instead of point

During training, we sample a random point \(z\) from this distribution and pass it to the decoder. Because the decoder must learn to reconstruct the original image from any point in that cloud, it learns a smooth, continuous mapping.

Step 2 — The Reparameterization Trick

You cannot backpropagate through a random sampling step. To fix this, VAEs use the reparameterization trick:

$$ z = \mu + \sigma \odot \epsilon $$

where \(\epsilon\) is random noise sampled from a standard normal distribution. The randomness is pushed to the side, allowing gradients to flow safely through \(\mu\) and \(\sigma\).

Walk it (Sampling from the latent cloud)

Suppose the encoder looks at a cat image and outputs a mean \(\mu = [0, 0]\) and standard deviation \(\sigma = [1, 1]\).

⚡ Exploring the Latent Manifold

Move the slider to travel along the \(z\) axis of a 1D latent space. The generated "image" (a simple curve) smoothly morphs from one shape to another, showing there are no "gaps" in the VAE latent space.

Where this shows up in ML

VAEs are a foundational generative model. They are used to generate new faces, create interpolations, and serve as the basis for more advanced models like Diffusion models.

Pitfall — blurry reconstructions. Because the VAE trains on probability clouds and uses Mean Squared Error for reconstruction, its outputs often look blurry compared to GANs. It is averaging out the details.

Bridge → Now we have a distribution, but how do we train the network to find it? We maximize the ELBO.

04 · Variational Autoencoders

Variational Lower Bound (ELBO)

To train a VAE, we want to maximize the likelihood of our data, \(p_\theta(x)\). But computing this directly is impossible (intractable) because we would have to check every possible latent variable \(z\).

Analogy first. We cannot measure the exact height of the mountain. But we can measure the height of a base camp that is always below the peak. If we push the base camp higher, we guarantee the peak is at least that high.

Step 1 — The ELBO

Instead of maximizing \(p_\theta(x)\), we maximize a tractable stand-in called the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO). The ELBO has two parts:

$$ \text{ELBO} = \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x)} \left[ \log p_\theta(x|z) \right] - D_{KL}(q_\phi(z|x) \parallel p(z)) $$

Step 2 — Two competing goals

1. Reconstruction loss: \(\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x)} \left[ \log p_\theta(x|z) \right]\). This rewards the model for accurately reconstructing the input \(x\) from the latent sample \(z\).

2. KL Divergence penalty: \(D_{KL}(q_\phi(z|x) \parallel p(z))\). This penalizes the encoder if its learned distribution \(q_\phi(z|x)\) strays too far from the prior \(p(z)\) (usually a standard normal distribution).

Walk it (Calculating the ELBO)

Assume the reconstruction loss is \(10\) (higher is better likelihood), and the KL penalty is \(2\).

Where this shows up in ML

The ELBO is the workhorse objective for variational inference across machine learning, appearing not just in VAEs but in Bayesian neural networks and diffusion models.

Pitfall — ignoring the KL term. Do not think of the KL term as just an error to minimize. It is a regularizer. Without it, the VAE would shrink the variance to zero and become a standard autoencoder, destroying the ability to generate new data.

Bridge → The ELBO gives us a good overall shape, but what if the features inside the latent space are tangled up? We can fix that with a small tweak.

05 · Advanced VAEs

Beta-VAE

The standard VAE is great, but its latent dimensions might mix features together. We want disentangled representations, where each dimension of \(z\) controls one interpretable feature.

Analogy first. Disentanglement is like having separate volume, bass, and treble knobs on a stereo. If the knobs were tangled, turning up the volume might also randomly change the bass. Beta-VAE forces the network to learn separate, independent knobs for the features.

Step 1 — Adding Beta

To force disentanglement, Beta-VAE (\(\beta\)-VAE) simply adds a multiplier, \(\beta\), to the KL-Divergence penalty in the ELBO:

$$ L = \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x)} \left[ \log p_\theta(x|z) \right] - \beta D_{KL}(q_\phi(z|x) \parallel p(z)) $$

When \(\beta = 1\), this is just a standard VAE. When \(\beta > 1\), we put stronger pressure on the latent distribution to match the independent, un-correlated prior. This heavy penalty forces the network to store information as efficiently as possible, aligning features with the independent axes of the latent space.

Walk it (Adjusting the Beta penalty)

Suppose the standard KL penalty is \(2.0\).

Where this shows up in ML

Disentangled representations are highly sought after for controllable generation and fairness. If a model disentangles "skin color" from "criminality prediction" or "lighting" from "identity," it is much safer and more useful to deploy.

Pitfall — ignoring reconstruction. If you make \(\beta\) too high, the network ignores the reconstruction loss completely. It will just output a perfect, independent Gaussian that decodes into blurry mush.

Bridge → Now our space is disentangled, but it is still continuous. What if we want to model things that are naturally discrete, like words?

06 · Advanced VAEs

VQ-VAE

Standard VAEs use continuous numbers. But many things in the real world are discrete. The Vector Quantized VAE (VQ-VAE) replaces the continuous latent space with a discrete set of codes.

Analogy first. Instead of giving exact RGB values, you just say "Blue." VQ-VAE snaps the messy output of the encoder to the nearest item in a dictionary.

Step 1 — The Dictionary

The VQ-VAE has a learned dictionary of vectors, \(e_k\). The encoder outputs a continuous vector, \(z_e(x)\). Then, we find the dictionary vector \(e_i\) that is closest to \(z_e(x)\) and pass \(e_i\) to the decoder.

Step 2 — The Stop-Gradient

The gradient cannot flow through this "snap to nearest" step. To fix this, VQ-VAE uses a stop-gradient operator, \(sg\). During the backward pass, it copies the gradients straight from the decoder to the encoder.

$$ L = \text{Reconstruction} + \| sg(z_e(x)) - e \|_2^2 + \beta \| z_e(x) - sg(e) \|_2^2 $$

Walk it (Snapping to nearest)

Let encoder output \(z = [0.2, 0.9]\). Dictionary vectors: \(e_1 = [0, 0]\), \(e_2 = [0, 1]\).

⚡ Nearest Neighbor Quantization

Move the encoder output \(z_e(x)\) (cyan dot). Watch how the VQ-VAE immediately snaps it to the nearest dictionary embedding \(e_k\) (pink squares).

Where this shows up in ML

VQ-VAEs are massive in modern AI. They are the foundation for generating high-quality speech, transferring voice styles, and are a key component in large text-to-image models.

Pitfall — prior training. Because the latent space is discrete, you can no longer sample by picking random numbers from a Gaussian. You must train a separate prior model (like PixelCNN) over the codes.

Bridge → VQ-VAE is great, but for high-resolution images, one bottleneck isn't enough.

07 · Advanced VAEs

VQ-VAE-2

The VQ-VAE-2 scales up the original idea by using a hierarchy of latent maps. Instead of one bottleneck, it has multiple layers of discrete codes.

Analogy first. First, you describe the big picture. "A woman with dark hair." Then you add details. "A small mole on her left cheek." The top layer handles global shape. The bottom handles details.

Step 1 — Hierarchical Maps

An image is compressed into a tiny map (e.g., 32×32) for global structure, and a larger map (e.g., 64×64) for local details. The decoder uses both maps to reconstruct the image. This approach allows VQ-VAE-2 to generate incredibly high-resolution, realistic images that rival GANs.

Walk it (Compression ratios)

Let the original image be \(256 \times 256 \times 3\) (color).

Where this shows up in ML

Hierarchical discrete autoencoders compress images for powerful autoregressive or diffusion models. They separate the heavy lifting of structure generation from painting fine pixels.

Pitfall — slow sampling. While VQ-VAE-2 avoids GAN mode collapse, generating an image requires running the autoregressive prior step-by-step over the discrete codes, which takes time.
